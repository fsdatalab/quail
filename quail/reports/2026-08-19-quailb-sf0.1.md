# QUAIL-B benchmark results at SF=0.1

Date: 2026-08-19. One H100 on Modal. Qwen3 4B fp8. This is the
rerun after the batched KV-write fix. The pre-fix numbers are in
`2026-08-18-quailb-sf0.1.md`. Plots are generated by
`reports/make_plots.py`. Data files are in `results/`.

## What QUAIL-B is

QUAIL-B is a benchmark of 14 queries over five document sets built
from real text (IMDB reviews, BioDEX patient reports, ABT-BUY product
descriptions, and derived sets). Each document has planted yes/no flag
lines with known ground truth, so we can measure both speed and
correctness.

The benchmark runs two passes in one session. The cold pass flushes
and disables the KV store (each document's KV is computed from
scratch). The warm pass enables the KV store (documents whose KV was
saved in a previous query can restore it instead of recomputing it).

SF (scale factor) controls document counts. At SF=0.1, the sets have
5,000 reviews (400 tokens each), 1,000 threads (1,200 tokens each),
200 reports (3,000 tokens each), 100 products (150 tokens each), and
2,560 medical terms (32 tokens each).

## Results

The plot `plots/quailb_cold_warm.png` shows each query's wall time
on both passes. The data is in `quailb_sf0.1_kvwrite.json`.

Cold pass total: 1,694.5 s, compared with 1,805.8 s before the
KV-write fix. Warm pass total: 1,633.6 s, compared with 1,717.8 s.
The warm pass was 61 s faster than the cold pass, which is 4%.

Per-query results:

| Query | What it does | Cold (s) | Warm (s) | Restored docs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 1 filter, 5k reviews | 31.2 | 31.7 | 0 |
| B2 | 5 filters, 5k reviews | 33.1 | 30.0 | 342 |
| B3w | 5 filters, written order | 32.2 | 29.5 | 342 |
| B3c | 5 filters, cost order | 32.0 | 29.5 | 342 |
| B4 | 2 filters, 200 reports (long docs) | 13.9 | 13.9 | 0 |
| B5 | 1 join, 200 reports x 2,560 terms | 274.2 | 274.6 | 0 |
| B6 | 1 filter + 1 join (pushdown) | 17.2 | 18.3 | 0 |
| B7 | 1 join, 500 reviews x 100 products | 42.4 | 42.5 | 0 |
| B8 | 1 exists-join, 1k threads x 100 products | 97.3 | 97.5 | 0 |
| B10 | 2 joins, chain with gating/dedup/replay | 332.7 | 332.5 | 0 |
| B11 | 2 joins, star shape | 311.6 | 312.0 | 0 |
| B12 | 1 filter + 1 join (two-sided pushdown) | 41.4 | 39.7 | 240 |

2,560-term partner list, with no early-stop, so they were left out.

Row counts and observed selectivities match the 2026-08-18 run.

## Compared with the pre-fix run

The filter queries (B1 to B4, B12, B13) took 216 s on the cold pass,
compared with 253 s before the fix. That is 14% faster. The 10k
filter gate had predicted about 9%. The extra saving is on B2 (33.1 s,
compared with 50.0 s) and B3c (32.0 s, compared with 42.5 s). Those
two queries issue more KV writes than a one-filter scan, so they
paid more for the old per-document `index_copy_` path.

The other queries (the joins, plus B6) took 1,350 s, compared with
1,382 s. That is 2% faster.

The worker container stayed up for both passes. Warm B2 restored 342
reviews and ran in 30.0 s, compared with 37.6 s last time, when a
restart had emptied the store.

## Why the warm pass was only 4% faster

The warm pass was honest, and the data shows where restored KV
helped and where it could not.

- Join queries do not use the store yet. B5, B8, B10, B11, and B14
  account for 1,290 s of the cold pass's 1,566 s of query time (82%).
  The KV store currently saves and restores only filter-scanned
  documents. Join anchors are not stored. B14 (a rerun of B5 with a
  new question) gained nothing: 274.5 s compared with 274.4 s cold.
  Adding join anchor storage is still the biggest remaining warm-pass
  improvement.
- The store held 342 of 5,000 reviews after warm B1. The reviews
  corpus is 3.2M tokens. At 144 KB of KV per token, the full set
  needs about 470 GB of host memory. The store has 64 GB. The planner
  keeps only documents whose KV is long enough that restoring it is
  faster than recomputing it, which selected the 342 longest reviews
  (432,525 tokens, about 63 GB). B2, B3w, and B3c each restored those
  342 documents. B2 went from 33.1 s to 30.0 s, B3w from 32.2 s to
  29.5 s, and B3c from 32.0 s to 29.5 s.
- Warm B6 then saved 240 thread documents (402,735 tokens). Last time
  it saved 18. That write filled the 64 GB store and evicted the
  reviews, so B13 restored 0 documents and ran in 32.7 s on both
  passes. B12 restored the 240 threads and went from 41.4 s to 39.7 s.
  We have not traced why the planner stored 240 threads this time
  instead of 18.
- Warm B1 saved the 342 reviews and was 0.5 s slower than cold B1
  (31.7 s compared with 31.2 s). Last time that save cost 5.8 s and
  the container then died. The save cost is smaller now, and the
  container stayed up.

## Selectivity check

The benchmark plants flag values with known rates, and each query
reports both the provided selectivity (what the planner was told) and
the observed selectivity (what the model actually returned). The model
was stricter than the planted labels on most filters.

For example, B3's first filter was planted at selectivity 0.9 (90% of
documents should pass), but the model only passed 40%. The 0.2 filter,
when asked first in B3c, observed 0.07 instead of 0.2.

Any cost model that uses provided selectivities to predict costs will
overestimate the number of surviving documents. The predictions are
still directionally useful (the low-selectivity filter still kills
more documents than the high-selectivity one), but the absolute
numbers drift.

## Cost order and written order are now the same speed

B3w (written order) and B3c (cost order, strictest filter first) run
the same five filters on the same 5,000 reviews. Before the KV-write
fix, B3c was 7 s slower on the cold pass (42.5 s compared with
35.4 s), even though it evaluated fewer document-stages (5,754
compared with 7,575).

After the fix, B3c is 32.0 s and B3w is 32.2 s. The stage counts are
unchanged. The old 7 s gap was the unbatched KV writes costing more
when stage 0 killed 93% of documents (B3c) than when it killed 60%
(B3w).

On the warm pass both orders are 29.5 s, because the 342 restored
documents skip that stage-0 compute.

## Follow-ups

- Wire join anchors into the KV store. Join queries are 82% of the
  suite and get no warm benefit today.
- Trace why warm B6 stored 240 thread documents this time, compared
  with 18 last time. That write evicted the reviews before B13.
- B9 and B15 still need the exists/anti early-stop before they are
  cheap enough to include.
