# Decision-2.0-Kai-0.6B against Qwen3-0.6B on QUAIL-B classification

Date: 2026-10-06.

Kai agrees with the reference labels on 63.1% of classified rows,
averaged over the 12 classification queries at sf 0.1, against 39.0%
for Qwen3-0.6B. Both models share one network, Qwen3-0.6B's. Qwen3-0.6B
takes 18% less total query time: 143.7 s against 174.5 s. The
comparison is of each model as Quail runs it today: Kai in the choice
format it was trained on, Qwen3-0.6B in Quail's generic classification
prompt with no chat template.

## Setup

- Runs, Quail only, one H100 per query family on Modal:
  - Kai: `/results/benchmarks/quailb/20261006T063809Z-90503923/`.
  - Qwen3-0.6B: `/results/benchmarks/quailb/20261006T063426Z-8704df00/`.
  - An earlier Kai run, `20261006T063425Z-eb7e46db`, was preempted on
    Modal and is incomplete; ignore it.
- Queries: IMDB-11 to IMDB-15, BIO-5, BIO-6, FEV-11, LEP-6, AGENT-3 to
  AGENT-5, the queries of the sf 1.0 Kai run in
  [fsdatalab/quail#184](https://github.com/fsdatalab/quail/pull/184).
  REL-AGENT-5, the 13th classification query at this pin, was left
  out.
- Scale factor 0.1, corpus `c_1aa2c4f0d0b6c816fd37aa5748c33341`,
  quail-b pin `f41e8aa`. Reference labels: collection
  `gt_72abc9af3feaea668e493ece67e980a0`, made by Qwen3 32B fp8.
- Code: `main` at `c590c3a` plus a `qwen3-0.6b-bf16` model spec
  (below). Query time excludes model startup. `$/query` uses
  $3.9492 per H100 hour.
- How each model classifies:
  - Kai: `decision_choice`. Its prompt writes one option block per
    label, and its head scores every option in one forward pass.
  - Qwen3-0.6B: the planner picks the label scoring rule by estimated
    cost. `letters` lists the labels as `- A: label` and scores the
    letter token after `ANSWER:`. `trie_decode` decodes greedily over
    the label tokens. `trie_tree` was not a candidate: tree attention
    is limited to models of at least 2B parameters.
  - Filters and joins inside these queries use each model's yes/no
    format: Kai's own, and the TRUE/FALSE prompt for Qwen3-0.6B.

## Prediction

- Kai at sf 0.1 within about 5 points of its sf 1.0 label agreement on
  each query.
- Qwen3-0.6B 10 to 20 points below Kai on average, and near chance on
  the AGENT queries.
- Query times within 1.5× of Kai's.

Against the prediction:

- Kai held. The largest change from sf 1.0 is AGENT-3, 53.4% against
  49.7%.
- Qwen3-0.6B is further behind than predicted: 24 points on the mean,
  not 10 to 20. The gap is largest with many labels: 34.5 to 37.4
  points on the single classifications with 21 or 26 labels, against
  12.6 to 19.9 points with 4 or 11 labels. AGENT-3 is the exception, a
  33.8-point gap with 5 labels.
- On AGENT-3, Qwen3-0.6B's 19.6% equals uniform chance over 5 labels,
  20%. AGENT-4 (5 and 4 labels) and AGENT-5 (5, 27, and 8 labels) are
  at 23.4% and 22.6%, pooled over their classifications.
  Majority-label baselines were not computed.
- Query time held except on LEP-6, where Qwen3-0.6B took 2.82 s
  against 16.01 s. Kai's prompt writes an option block for each of the
  15 labels, so it reads more tokens per document: 19.6M input tokens
  against 6.8M. The same holds on BIO-5, 624,092 against 243,166 for
  26 labels.

## What is not settled

- The two models do not get equally suited prompts. Kai runs its
  trained format. Qwen3-0.6B gets raw text with no chat template, and
  a scoring rule chosen for cost, not accuracy. Rerunning IMDB-11,
  BIO-5, and AGENT-3 with the rule forced to `letters` and then to
  `trie_decode`, with and without the Qwen3 chat template, would show
  how much of the gap is the prompt.
- The reference is Qwen3 32B's labels, not human labels.
- One run per model, at sf 0.1 only.
